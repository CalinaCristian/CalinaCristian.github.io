01

What the player sees

The résumé is the route.

Drive between career chapters in Bucharest, fly from Otopeni to SeaTac, and finish in Bellevue. The classic CV stays one click away.

Story

Ten chapters, eight districts

Each chapter has a real street start, a local route and a CV destination. Offices contain distinct interiors, CV screens and a teacher, professor or manager with A–D (or 1–4) conversation choices. A map montage connects distant districts; the flight is a globe journey, not a continuous drive across the world.

Free roam

The story can wait

Continue appears after a saved session exists; fresh-menu and persisted-save availability are checked separately at desktop and phone sizes. The World map opens districts and tourist scenes. Leaving the story preserves its progress; Resume story restores the objective and chapter flow. Here, București, Seattle, Bellevue and World tabs provide local navigation and regional travel.

Driving includes fuel, mapped stations, speed limits and enforcement, honking, traffic reactions and a radio fact ticker. On foot you can walk, run, jump, enter an office, find the parked car or call it to the kerb. Illegal parking can lead to a ticket, clamp or tow. Tourist visits add plaques, conversations, postcard challenges, short tours and activities.

Photos, drone, progression and persistence

Photo mode crops the rendered frame to a 3:2 viewfinder, supports zoom, a thirds grid and Natural / Warm / B&W / Film filters, then composes a captioned postcard. Pictures stay in the local gallery until the player chooses Save or Share. They are not uploaded by the game.

The drone launches outdoors while on foot, including tourist scenes. Cine, Normal and Sport modes use 4 / 12 / 26 m/s horizontal speeds, with a Shift boost. Limits are simulated: 120 m above ground in metric mode or 400 ft (121.9 m) in imperial mode, 800 m range in districts or 450 m in spots, and about ten minutes of hover battery, with return-home at 15%. “Go here” moves the player to the position below the drone. A hidden Dinamo interaction connects the drone with a physical football; the source links explain it, so this guide leaves the discovery intact.

Completion weights story chapters at 40%, CV answers at 10%, tourist visits at 15%, postcards at 10%, and achievements at 25%. The inspected catalogue contains 112 achievements; the cycling activity is counted dynamically when available. A catalogue count does not mean every entry is active at first launch.

Continue stores the player’s district, story/free-roam state, car and walking positions, fuel, stats and selected conditions. Traffic and pedestrian positions are regenerated; this is a session restore rather than a complete world snapshot. Photo mode resumes closed.

OpenStreetMap is the geographic base. Aerial imagery contributes roof, canopy and green-ground measurements. Street View is a side-by-side visual check only; it does not supply extracted game data, textures or committed reference pictures.

1. Geographic data, scale and district boundaries

OSM ways and relations supply roads, lanes, one-ways, speed limits, bridge/tunnel layers, building footprints, mapped courtyards, land use, water, trees, lamps, businesses and fuel stations. Missing tags remain a source of uncertainty. Research fills selected gaps; explicit OSM tags take precedence over measurements, then estimates.

District worlds use real metres at 1:1 scale: x points east, z south and y up. Latitude/longitude project around a city anchor. Legacy city-wide corridor JSON and its compression still exist in the repository; the chapter districts are the current experience.

Playable boundaries combine buffered chapter routes, circles and polygons. Roads cut by the boundary receive closures and story hints. Reviewed Basarab approach/return corridors now retain their actual mapped connections; complete closure, driving and legal-navigation checks remain pending. Raw motor turn restrictions are catalogued for all eight districts. GPS and NPC choices enforce the supported node-via rules; malformed, conditional and unsupported cases remain explicit. Fresh native routing and full-body traffic checks remain required. Pasaj Unirii’s mapped surface approaches and ramp exits are also restored where the old district edge cut them off. The source expansion retains prior buildings, heights, courtyards and measured trees, and keeps new literal foreground rail runs. Full native passage and actor-clearance review remains open. Retained transit metadata distinguishes vehicle stop positions from mapped platforms. A proven vehicle-stop point no longer locates a guessed shelter unit: its inferred roof, glass, bench, pole, sign and matching colliders are withheld while the real shelter geometry remains unknown. Literal no-shelter tags take precedence; missing metadata stays explicitly unreviewed. Mapped roofs, platforms and stops remain intact. Fresh native body, route and visual checks are still required. The nearby 600 m scenery ring retains mapped streets and buildings but adds no continuous drivable network or moving traffic. A nearby district is not loaded as a second simulated world: its visible context comes from the current district’s source ring and explicitly placed landmarks. More distant simplified masses can remain visible from a high drone. The Seattle tourist rebuild replaces procedural scattered context in Pike Place and Seattle Center with literal source buildings, USGS terrain and mapped water/trees. Its roughly 27 MiB compressed source/cache candidate has an exact-kernel rebake. Actual native checks exposed slow cold construction, an over-budget elevated view and remaining visual defects. Tree detail now follows the camera’s actual world position and height without removing mapped trees; new native budget and visual checks remain required. Unknown elevated parts and unresolved structural data remain listed explicitly. Flight is limited to the playable union plus 150 m, including the full Dinamo stadium; that margin is not a promise of off-district driving.

2. Offline preparation and compact JSON

tools/osm/build-data.mjs processes downloaded OSM tiles and research dossiers into game/data/<city>-<district>.json. It prepares the road graph, usable district geometry, buildings, areas, scenery streets and lookup tables. Places are separately indexed for map categories and reachable navigation points.

Schema v2 keeps compact integer decimetre arrays and indexed strings, with precise metre-space sidecars for source road nodes, building footprints, courtyard rings and physical dimensions. Optional parallel metadata keeps OSM identities, height-source classifications, roof measurements, building types, parking side/orientation, tree species and aerial survey coverage. Mapped inner rings remain open courtyards in rendering and collision. Mapped parts and their parent outline retain all source identities; shared surface ownership renders overlapping volume once while preserving uncovered residual geometry. Roof profiles distinguish literal total heights from estimated wall/eave heights, and retain unsupported source shapes as explicit gaps.

Not every source footprint is a custom model. Default heights and roof rules fill gaps; aerial roof classes can be uncertain. A source-domain expansion retains explicitly reviewed prior fallback models for unchanged buildings, including their unknown estimated heights and roofs. Newly matched business names cannot silently resize those buildings; literal tags and new measurements still take precedence. The inventory and audit tooling make these decisions inspectable rather than turning them into a claim of exact reconstruction.

3. Browser assembly and delivery

game/index.html loads main.js through native ES modules. world.js fetches the selected district JSON and builds roads, grade-aware junctions, sidewalks, markings, buildings, vegetation, props, water, closures and custom landmarks. The navigation graph and collision data are built alongside the visible world.

The renderer uses vendored Three.js r170 and vendored post-processing modules. The game has no bundler, application server, login service or runtime AI generation. GitHub Pages serves static HTML, CSS, JavaScript and JSON; Node scripts and Playwright are development tools. A weather API request is separate from static asset delivery.

Simulated

What they do

Traffic flow, pedestrians, conversations, enforcement, fuel use, collisions, drone limits and activity progress.

Live / fallback

What the sky does

Device date and city time zone, computed sun and moon, API weather or deterministic climate-based fallback.

Traffic, signals and navigation

Traffic is browser-side, seeded rule-based simulation. Road geometry supplies lane centres, directions, permitted edges, speed limits and grade. Vehicles choose routes, follow leaders, change lanes, yield at junctions and crossings, and react to the player. Source road nodes stay fixed; literal widths and independently measured asymmetric carriageways define physical lane centres. Two legal directions may share one narrow physical lane, so reservations hold opposing traffic outside the mouths until the rear clears. Private/restricted source roads remain visible while legal access controls public navigation and ambient traffic. Buses, trolleybuses and trams use country-specific models and mapped or researched route constraints. This is not live Google traffic or a feed of real vehicle positions.

Local-hour profiles change density, parking and aggression. Researched congestion hotspots add rush-hour standstill behavior; a time override changes the traffic hour too. Signal phases are assigned from junction approaches to avoid conflicting movements, with clearance handling and pedestrian recovery. Simulated cycle timing is not a municipal live signal feed.

nav.js uses A* routing on travel time over the loaded road graph, respects directions and adds route costs, then draws dashboard instructions. A spatial edge index supports nearby-road queries and recovery. The map distinguishes a place’s geographic location from its reachable street endpoint; the last stretch may need walking. Selecting a pin or clicking the map marks the destination and can set a local route. Hold F for 1.2 seconds, or choose Fast travel, to move. A remote destination remains marked without inventing a route across unloaded districts.

Driving, walking, collisions and fuel

The driving controller models acceleration, braking, reverse steering, grip, road grade and contact. Its collision body follows the visible cockpit’s bonnet-to-boot footprint, offset from the eye position. Lane-drop lookahead follows the lane inherited through each future road leg, while retaining genuine merge waits and red-light queues. Buildings, landmarks, props, walls, vehicles and pedestrians use specialized collision checks; mapped holes and vertical bands matter for courtyards, bridges, stands and roofs. This is purpose-built game physics rather than a universal rigid-body engine.

Bridge and underpass corrections are the current priority. Basarab now uses one reviewed model for road, rail and junction heights, including finite approaches and closed deck undersides. Marked player road queries follow that same field; deck collision probes use the actual pitched cockpit height at each probe, with the normal wall and roof guards retained. The northern model now follows the complete mapped descent to the real ground junction, using an explicitly estimated ramp timing and retaining the other grade-separated streets. CPU replay of the actual combined source solver reaches ground; a full native approach-to-ground drive is still required. These heights remain estimates; fresh combined construction, full body clearance, continuous driving and full-size driver, side and mirror views are still required. A render-ownership candidate removes only duplicated inferred parapet face portions inside existing bridge guards while retaining physical and mapped barriers. Its Float32 residuals are explicit; fresh bridge views remain required. The separate Coposu covered-driveway correction preserves the mapped canopy while removing its false underground street profile. True tunnel roof slabs now sit below their retained overhead surfaces, with matching physical bands and color-preserving depth occlusion. Structural and isolated GPU controls pass. Roof ownership now uses the same final retained small-water topology as the renderer: six raw-only refusals clear in CPU replay while all106prior roof models remain unchanged. Genuine retained-water intersections remain unresolved. Public covered passage, portals and full driver views remain under review; the latest native journey stopped behind a mispositioned parked van on the ramp. The creation fix now validates literal parking support and whole-body road, pedestrian and static clearance before marking a generated car parked. A refused parking choice keeps that same new car driving from its original source lane and station, with the original fleet and random sequence. The exact 489-car source replay preserves all nine original generated slots; fresh native launch, passage and budget checks remain required. Closure warnings and nearby-closure impact classification now use the normalized physical barrier height band: a ground roadwork cannot announce a blockage on a bridge above it or soften an unrelated collision there. Crew chatter also stays on its associated road level. Posted source height/width limits now filter routes using the upright emitted vehicle model, including trolley poles and taxi signs. Incompatible initial actors are reported explicitly; actual traffic and clearance acceptance remain separate. Historical pictures retain their original source and do not certify these changes.

The on-foot controller is reused in districts, offices and tourist scenes. Ground queries support raised sidewalks, steps and scene surfaces; colliders and extra obstacles constrain movement. Pedestrians have seasonal clothing and props, avoidance, reactions and knock-down/recovery behavior. They are simulated agents, not recorded people.

Only OSM amenity=fuel locations create stations. The canopy is procedural decoration placed only where it fits; real buildings are not moved to make space. Districts without a mapped station do not consume fuel during driving or local fast travel. Speed and red-light cameras use mapped placements and game rules. Fuel prices, consumption, fines and towing costs are game constants/rules rather than live prices; the display uses local currencies and litres or US gallons.

CV offices, school and university interiors

Four room candidates now use original geometry guided by authenticated photographs or official tours: Arnia Europe House, Deutsche Bank BOB, Rosetti classrooms and the Politehnica Rectorat cupola hall. Source-led materials and furniture replace the earlier generic interpretations. Selected room layouts and dimensions remain estimates; employment-era appearance, exact floor and facade bearing are unresolved. GTS, Luxoft and both UiPath rooms retain explicit source gaps. Actual photographs, rendered design proposals and unrelated buildings are distinguished in the interior research ledger. Provider photographs remain private comparison material. Off-axis camera and bounded render-target helpers now have independent geometry/resource controls; the same retained scene can advance its environment without drawing another world. Actual room floor and facing direction remain unresolved, and live window integration is still pending. Native desktop/phone visual checks, live geographically correct window views and repeated-room GPU resource verification are still required; constructor tests do not establish those results.

Time, live weather, seasons and environmental effects

The clock starts from the device’s date/time and the current city’s IANA time zone. Solar calculations use latitude, longitude and the simulated date. Numeric hour overrides preserve a local hour; named lighting presets can override sun appearance. Conditions follow district/story travel and Continue. Climate and time are local to the destination when live/automatic modes are selected.

Open-Meteo provides current weather, cached per location in sessionStorage for 15 minutes, with an eight-second request timeout. Rounded 1991–2020 climate normals provide a deterministic fallback when live data is unavailable. Fallback weather is plausible synthetic weather, not an observation. Explicit presets bypass the live choice.

Weather transitions ease over roughly ten seconds. Rain and snow particles run mainly in shaders; wet roads, puddles, snow cover, fog/visibility, headlight and lamp reflections, wipers, cloud cover and weather-dependent grip respond to conditions. Roads can stay wet after rain. Seasons change foliage, grass, leafless crowns, clothing and selected activities; snow appears on streets, roofs, grounds and landmark surfaces. Fountain operation also follows a local seasonal schedule. These are visual and gameplay approximations, not hydrology or a climate model.

Audio, local saves, privacy and telemetry

Web Audio synthesizes engine, horn, weather, interaction and drone sounds, with scene ambience and selectable volume/mute. Audio begins after a user gesture. The fact ticker and dialogue come from curated game data, not live AI conversations.

IndexedDB database dmcv stores the session, key-value mirrors and the newest 60 photo blobs. Options and progression still use synchronous localStorage keys mirrored by the save layer. Autosaves run every 30 seconds and at important transitions, hiding and page exit. Storage failures fall back to local storage or memory; browser clearing/private-mode policies can lose progress. There are no accounts or cloud saves.

Production analytics use cookieless PostHog EU, respecting Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. Telemetry reports game events and selected aggregate state; it does not turn local photos into uploads. The overview itself does not load the game’s telemetry script.

These are test budgets and quality goals, not universal performance guarantees. Hardware, viewport, weather, scene, GPU driver and quality tier change the result. Software-rendered CI measurements do not establish real-device frame rates.

Geometry, culling and shared draw work

World cell geometry is merged by material and regrouped into larger cells to reduce draw calls. Buildings use facade atlases/shader variation instead of a mesh for each window. Landmarks merge their geometry by material. Traffic, crowds and parked cars use instancing and distance-based detail; nearby trees have a richer LOD while far crowns use cheaper geometry and restricted shadows.

Range culling and camera frustum culling avoid drawing invisible chunks. Large water surfaces are split into spatial meshes. Source scenery remains world geometry and can be visible outside the driving core; “outside playable” does not mean “off screen” or a static painted backdrop. Shadow spans and caster selection are bounded to keep nearby streets sharp without shadowing an entire city.

Lighting, materials, post-processing and mirror

Renderer3D combines a procedural sky, sun/moon, image-based environment reflections, a spherical-harmonic ambient light probe, night emissive materials, pooled street lights and headlights. AgX tone mapping is the default. Weather modifies fog using distance-based aerial perspective and patches eligible surfaces for wetness and snow. Water uses shader ripples, Fresnel/environment reflection, shoreline foam and lamp streaks without a separate reflection render.

The rear-view mirror renders a second camera into a texture. High tiers update it every other frame, Medium every third frame; Low/low internal tiers disable it. Three warm-up frames refresh it after placement. The main camera includes cockpit layer 1; the mirror renders the world without the player’s cockpit. A mirror, shadows and post-processing can multiply rendering work, so a mesh-only triangle count is incomplete evidence.

Sharp car displays at every graphics tier

The instrument cluster and navigation/media screen are redrawn after world antialiasing, so lower scene resolution and FXAA do not soften their text. Only their existing physical surfaces are drawn at bounded output resolution; the wheel, dashboard and other obstructions still cover them using the original scene depth. Camera positions and tap/click coordinates stay the same. The mirror uses the same protection when enabled; Low keeps its existing mirror policy.

World targets retain their adaptive pixel ratio. Additional display presentation is limited to device pixel ratio 3 and 4 million pixels, without lowering an already higher selected world resolution. Low keeps multisample antialiasing on its smaller world target, while the large presentation buffer avoids multisample storage. Auxiliary resources are prewarmed, released with their owner, and included in complete-frame draw and triangle counts.

Tours, maps, activities and what is actually complete

A SpotSession hosts the walker, scene weather/clock, crowds, plaques, spot map, objectives, interaction prompts, postcard challenge and tour UI. Short tours require an available activity first and use at most three stops and 300 m of walkable routing. This includes the measured 209 m approach to the Cișmigiu table that the former limit excluded. If no safe activity route fits, the tour does not start. Skip step (J) and End tour remain available without awarding skipped progress. Tap a destination or the map to set a waypoint. A blue walking route, direction and distance persist after closing the map; gold follows the guide. Clear removes the waypoint. Routes on raised floors stay on actual supporting surfaces; a gallery void cannot borrow a path on the ground below. Destinations retain their floor, and reaching the same map position on another floor is not arrival. Routes between storeys are not yet solved: use the stairs, then the route updates on your new floor. On reaching an activity, a nearby guide or conversation yields its interaction prompt to the reachable current task. Other physical targets, actual reach, enabled conditions and vertical range retain their normal checks. Opening a modal activity retains the itinerary; its completion advances the matching step, while cancelling it leaves the step unfinished. Small screens keep the current step and Skip/End above the activity panel; activity notifications flow below its controls. Ordinary arrival messages also move below the measured title/tour card when their horizontal spans overlap. Global map, drone and movement controls yield while playing, leaving the activity and tour controls accessible.

The Arc rebuild includes an opponent race and timed baguette catches. Spec-driven development with AI

The spec is the shared memory.
The evidence is the handoff.

AI agents implement bounded work with fresh context. A human owns the goals and review decisions; a coordinating root integrates and verifies the result.

  1. 01

    Human intent → explicit requirements

    The owner sets the experience, realism bar and priorities. 00-vision.md records product goals; focused specs turn them into behaviors and acceptance criteria.

  2. 02

    AGENTS + contracts → a bounded task

    Agents read repository instructions, the architecture, status and relevant specs. File ownership and module contracts limit overlapping changes. Source code resolves whether an old spec statement is still true.

  3. 03

    Fresh context → branch + isolated worktree

    A delegated worker researches and edits an assigned slice in its own checkout. Parallel workers can handle independent modules, while shared-file changes go through the owner/coordinator. Isolated snapshots keep a running browser test from seeing half-edited code.

  4. 04

    Implementation → checks + pictures

    Syntax/logic checks, browser regression scenarios, performance counts and targeted gameplay tests catch different failures. Visual harnesses render chapter routes and spots; screenshots must be inspected. Geographic audits compare retained source identities, footprints and source metadata.

  5. 05

    Evidence → root integration → release

    The handoff names its commit, changed behavior, test results, screenshots and remaining gaps. The root resolves integration, runs combined checks and updates the README/specs. A separately authorized deployment publishes static files from master through GitHub Pages.

What the AI does—and what the workflow does not claim

Agents can research documents, inspect code, implement geometry and behavior, run automated browsers, compare images and keep the specification trail current. They do not make the city at runtime or replace measured sources with generated guesses. A seeded simulation and procedural model are ordinary program code even when AI helped write them.

This describes the repository’s instructed workflow and the current coordinated work, not a guarantee that every historical commit followed every step. Native persistent/background goal tools are unavailable in this session; ongoing work is coordinated through explicit agent tasks and handoffs, not a claimed unattended background goal engine.

Specs contain aspirations, dated reviews and unresolved requests. “Review” is not “done.” The process is strongest when it preserves contrary evidence: screenshots with defects, missing source fields, tests that fail, and limits that survive a release.

QA entry points and what each establishes
Logic harness
npm run harness drives scenarios such as chapters, traffic and on-foot movement. Required regression slices aim for 0 high / 0 medium findings.
Visual harness
npm run harness:visual samples route views, detects candidate geometry/text defects and records render budgets. Human/agent screenshot review is still required.
Spot harness
npm run harness:spots loads scenes, walks spawns and audits geometry/collision. A load pass does not establish visual fidelity.
Focused checks
Conditions persistence, map marking/held-F travel, signals, courtyard collision, football physics and short tourist tours each have dedicated regression scripts.
Reality comparison
Street-level camera pairs and top-down aerial tiles expose mismatches at matched poses. Camera altitude and imagery dates can differ, so grade ambiguity, season and source revision must remain visible.

No tests are run merely by opening this overview. Its own verification checks document layout, links, search, generated inventory and accessibility behavior; gameplay evidence belongs to the corresponding game release.

Known limits that matter when reading the guide
  • Missing OSM tags, approximate heights, inferred vegetation and procedural facades remain. Measured footprints do not make every visible surface exact.
  • All eight districts have their own source audits and complete aerial capture collections. Coverage does not establish uniform visual quality: historic facades, complex roofs, tree crowns, local grade and covered structures retain explicit review gaps.
  • Districts, tourist scenes and legacy corridors have different geometry paths. A shared model API is not universal parity across them.
  • Historical spot review gates and old performance tables are dated evidence. Current source counts and targets are kept separate from measured present-day success.
  • Day/night and weather are local simulations driven by clock/API inputs. Traffic, congestion, rules and NPC conversations are synthetic.
  • Local browser storage can be cleared. No cloud service preserves an account, photo album or full traffic snapshot.
