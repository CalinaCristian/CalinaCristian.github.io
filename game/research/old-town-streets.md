## Strada Lipscani
- Length: about 1 km in OSM (13 ways); the historic-centre stretch runs from Calea Victoriei to Calea Moșilor; OSM surface: asphalt, paved, paving_stones, sett
- Named after the "lipscani", merchants selling goods brought from Leipzig (Lipsca); a lane since the early 18th century.
- Eclectic late-19th/early-20th-c. fronts (Renaissance, baroque, rococo and neoclassical elements). Nationalised 1948, badly degraded after the 1980s; restored and repaved in the early 2010s, when ruins of medieval inns were found under the paving.
- Notable fronts: Banca Berliner Gesellschaft (1910–13); Hanul Gabroveni (1739, rebuilt, entrance from Lipscani); Hanul cu Tei (1833, a passage to Blănari); Cărturești Carusel at no. 55 (an 1860s bank building, bookshop since 2015); the National Bank (BNR) old palace on the S side between Smârdan and Doamnei.
- Today a pedestrian street of bars, restaurants and some chain shops.
- Venues mapped within 18 m: 73, 14 with a terrace. ## Strada Gabroveni
- Length: 220 m (ro.wikipedia) / 231 m OSM, from Șelari to Bd. I.C. Brătianu with a slight right-hand bend; Strada Zarafi to Lipscani at about 110 m, Pasajul Francez to Covaci 40 m further on; OSM surface: paved
- Named after Gabrovo (Bulgaria) knife merchants.
- I.L. Caragiale opened the "Berăria Mihalcea și Caragiale" here in 1893.
- Mostly late-19th/early-20th-c. buildings; the whole street is a listed architectural ensemble (LMI B-II-a-B-18798). After rehabilitation: shops, pubs, fast food and pizzerias.

## Strada Smârdan

## Landmarks along these streets
- **Hanul cu Tei**: Built 1833 by Anastasie Hagi Gheorghe Polizu and Ștefan Popovici. The only Bucharest inn kept in its original form. A 75 m north–south passage between Lipscani and Blănari: shops over vaulted cellars (14 per owner) and richly ornamented iron gates at both ends. The owners' monogram "A.P." and "S.P." is visible at the Blănari entrance, and "1833" is carved over the entrance. Today art galleries, plus a bar in the cellar. (https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanul_cu_Tei, https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strada_Lipscani)
- **Hanul lui Manuc**: Built 1806–1808 by Manuc Bei; the 1812 Treaty of Bucharest was signed here. Four large wings joined by two tiers of wooden galleries (arcaded ground floor, wooden gallery with balustrades above) around a courtyard. A grand staircase sits under a kiosk with a pointed roof and projecting rafters. Historically 15 vaulted cellars, 23 shops on the ground floor and 107 rooms upstairs. Restored by the Cantacuzino heirs (2012). Today restaurants, cafés and a courtyard terrace; Franceză 62 (LMI B-II-m-A-18788). (https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanul_lui_Manuc, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuc%27s_Inn)
- **Curtea Veche**: The first princely court of Bucharest (probably from Mircea cel Bătrân, late 14th/early 15th c.). Vlad III dated a document from his "fortress of Bucharest" here on 20 September 1459. Unused after the 1718 fire and 1738 earthquake. The ruins of the voivodal palace are now a protected archaeological site and the Curtea Veche Museum. The Buna Vestire church (1559) is the oldest church in Bucharest kept in its original form. The court spread between Halelor, Șelari, Lipscani, Bărăției and Calea Moșilor. (https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtea_Veche, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtea_Veche)

**Look across all six streets** (Wikipedia descriptions plus OSM):
- 1–3-storey late-19th/early-20th-c. eclectic and neoclassical fronts with cornices, wrought-iron balconies and arched ground-floor shop openings; a mix of restored and decaying plaster.
- Pedestrian, paved or cobbled (Covaci is setts).
- Dense terraces on Smârdan, Șelari, Lipscani and Covaci.
